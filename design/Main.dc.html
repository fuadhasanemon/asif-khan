About

My research asks how public institutions decide who is counted, how people are classified, and who is held accountable when those decisions go wrong.

I completed an M.A. in Political Science at Western Illinois University in 2026 with a 4.00 GPA, after a B.S.S. in International Relations at the University of Dhaka. At Western Illinois I worked as a teaching support assistant in the Department of Political Science and as a pre-college instructor in the Rural Postsecondary and Economic Development (RPED) Program.

My interests sit where immigration policy, GovTech, and AI in public administration meet. I work with survey and interview data, comparative case studies, and quantitative tools in R, Python, and SPSS.

I grew up in Bangladesh and now live in Waukegan, Illinois. I speak Bengali, English, Hindi, and Urdu.

Research

Three lines of work, each asking how institutions exercise authority over the people they count, admit, or process. Choose one to explore it.

Counting and institutional authority

How official numbers are produced, contested, and carried into politics. My sole-authored paper examines the death toll of 1971 and the apology demand in Bangladesh–Pakistan relations since then.

  • Genocide, numbers, and memory: The death toll and the apology demand in Bangladesh–Pakistan relations since 1971Accepted, MPSA 2027

Migration policy and the limits of state authority

How states organise labour migration and admission, and where their reach ends. Current work studies Bangladesh–South Korea labour cooperation beyond the Employment Permit System and compares immigration systems in the United States and Canada.

  • Beyond EPS: Bangladesh–South Korea labor cooperation under demographic pressure through migration diplomacy, corridor reliability, and institutional upgradingAccepted, MPSA 2027
  • Comparative immigration governance in the United States and CanadaIndependent project, 2025

AI, GovTech, and public administration

How human oversight works when government adopts automated and digital tools. I study accountability in federal AI workflows and how universities adopt new digital systems under shared decision-making.

  • Speed, verification, and accountability: Human oversight beyond formal authority in federal AI workflowsSole author, in preparation
  • Governing digital transformation under shared governance: Accountability and adoption in UIUC’s Canvas transitionIndependent project, 2026
  • A governance-ready project business case for a neurodiversity partnership with Western Illinois UniversityCapstone, 2025

Earlier work in computational text analysis

Before political science, I coauthored peer-reviewed work on sentiment analysis and applied machine learning, including a first-authored review of sentiment analysis datasets in PeerJ Computer Science. Listed under Publications.
